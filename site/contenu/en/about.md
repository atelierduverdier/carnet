## Why in public

These notes already existed, scattered across files only I can find. Publishing them costs almost nothing more and changes one thing: what you write for someone else, you write better. You can no longer get away with "the libdrm thing" — you have to name the library, the version, and the symbol that was missing.

The other reason is more selfish. A fault you solve and do not write down is a fault you will pay for twice. I have already paid twice for the same one, a month apart, the second time convinced I had fixed it.

## What it is written on

- **Arch Linux**, CachyOS flavour, rolling;
- **Plasma** (KDE) on Wayland, single screen, dark background;
- an **AMD** graphics card on the Mesa/radeonsi driver — several notes depend on this, and the fault in question does not exist on NVIDIA;
- a **CNC router** driven by LinuxCNC on a second machine, a laser module on the head, a large-format 3D printer.

One detail that matters: this machine is used to **make things**. When a choice comes down to elegance versus the spindle turning tomorrow morning, the spindle wins. Some of the notes show it.

## What you will not find

- **No distribution comparisons.** There are plenty elsewhere, and mine would be an account of a habit, not of a measurement.
- **No "top 10 commands".** A command without the problem it solves does not stick.
- **No untried solutions.** If I did not run it, it is not here. When I pass on a lead without having verified it, it says so in plain words.
- **No personal configuration copied verbatim.** Paths, machine names and credentials are replaced by examples. What matters is the method.

## Mistakes

A wrong note is worse than a missing one: it wastes your time with authority. If you find one, write — the address is at the foot of every page. I fix the note rather than adding a comment, and I say what changed.
